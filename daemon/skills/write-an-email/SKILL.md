# Write an email

The goal is a message the reader understands on the first read and can answer in one go.

## Before you write

You need four things. Take them from what the person said; ask only for what is missing, in one
short question.

1. **Who reads it**, and how well they know the sender (a friend, a colleague, a stranger, an
   office).
2. **What the reader should do** after reading: answer a question, agree, pay, come, know
   something. One main thing.
3. **The facts** it must carry: dates, amounts, names, places, links.
4. **The language and the tone**: write in the language the person writes to you in unless told
   otherwise.

If you are replying to a message, read it first and list every question in it. The reply answers
each of them.

## Steps

1. Write the subject last, but put it first: five to eight words that say what the email is and
   what is needed. "Invoice 114: payment date?" not "Question".
2. Open with one line of greeting that fits how well they know each other.
3. Put the request or the news in the first two sentences. Background comes after, and only what
   the reader needs.
4. Give the facts plainly. A date is written out with its weekday. An amount has its currency.
   Several items become a short list.
5. Say exactly what you ask for and by when. Offer the easy answer: "A yes is enough."
6. Close with one line and the sender's name. Leave a place for the name if you do not know it.
7. Read it as the receiver. Cut every sentence they would skip.

## Rules

- Short: most emails fit in five to eight sentences. A first message to a stranger fits in five.
- One topic in one email.
- Polite and direct. No "I hope this email finds you well", no "just", no stack of sorry.
- Say no clearly when the answer is no, with one reason and, if there is one, another offer.
- For a complaint: what happened, when, what you want done. No insults.
- Never invent a fact, a name, a number or a promise. Mark what you do not know like this:
  [date], [amount].
- Do not send anything. You write the text; the person sends it.

## The answer

Give the email ready to copy:

- a line that starts with `Subject:`;
- an empty line, then the body.

After it, add one short line only if something is still open: what you left in brackets, or what
you assumed.
