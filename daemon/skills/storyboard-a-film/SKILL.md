# Storyboard a film

The goal is a list of shots for Studio. Each shot becomes one short clip, made on its own by a
video model that has never seen the other shots. So every shot description must carry everything
that should look the same from shot to shot.

## Steps

1. **Get the idea in one sentence**: who, where, what happens, how it ends. Ask one question only
   if there is no story to tell yet. If no length was given, plan six shots.
2. **Write the cast sheet.** For each character, one fixed description of 12 to 20 words: age,
   build, hair, clothes with colours, one thing that stands out. Give each a short name.
   Example: MARA: woman in her thirties, short black hair, yellow raincoat, red rubber boots,
   small silver earring.
3. **Write the place sheet.** For each place, one fixed description of 10 to 15 words: what it
   is, the time of day, the light, the weather, two things in it.
4. **Choose one look** for the whole film, in one line: for example "soft hand-held film look,
   muted colours, shallow focus". This line goes into every shot.
5. **Split the story into shots.** One shot shows one action in one place, three to five seconds
   long. A change of place or time is a new shot.
6. **Write each shot** as one paragraph in English, in this order:
   - the framing: wide shot, medium shot, close-up, over the shoulder;
   - the character, with the full description from the cast sheet, word for word;
   - what they do, as one movement you can see;
   - the place, with the full description from the place sheet, word for word;
   - how the camera moves: still, slow push in, pan left, follows;
   - the look line.
7. **Check each shot alone.** Cover the other shots and read it. Could a stranger draw it? If it
   says "she", "the same room" or "as before", put the full description back in.

## Rules

- Describe what is seen, not what is felt or meant. "She wipes her eyes", not "she is sad".
- One action for each shot. Two actions become two shots.
- No words spoken, no text on screen, no logos: video models draw them badly.
- At most two characters in a shot. Crowds and hands that hold small things often go wrong.
- Do not change a character's clothes or a place's light unless the story says so. If it does,
  write a new sheet entry with a new name (MARA, WET).
- Keep each shot between 40 and 80 words.
- Do not name real people or film characters that belong to someone.

## The answer

- **Idea**: one sentence.
- **Look**: the one line.
- **Cast**: name and fixed description, one line each.
- **Places**: name and fixed description, one line each.
- **Shots**: numbered. For each: a three-word title, the seconds, and the full paragraph, ready
  to paste into a shot in Studio.

End with one line: which shot to make first to test the look.
