# Summarise a document

The goal is a summary the author would agree with and a busy reader can act on.

## Steps

1. Find out who the summary is for and how long it should be. If the person did not say, write
   for someone who has not read the text, and aim for about one tenth of its length, never more
   than 250 words.
2. Read the whole text before you write. If it is in a file, read it in pieces to the end; a
   summary of the first half is wrong. If the text was cut off, say where it stops.
3. Write down the answer to one question: what is this text for? (To report, to argue, to
   decide, to teach.) The summary is built around that.
4. Collect the points, in the text's own order:
   - the main claim or result;
   - the three to five things that support it, with their numbers, names and dates;
   - what was decided, and who does what by when;
   - what the text itself says is open, uncertain or disputed.
5. Write the summary from those notes, not from the text. Use your own plain words. Keep numbers,
   names and dates exactly as written.
6. Check every sentence against the text: where does it say this? Delete what you cannot point
   to. Then check the other way: is a main point of the text missing?

## Rules

- Add nothing: no opinion, no advice, no background the text does not give.
- Keep the weight of the original. A side remark stays a side remark or goes.
- Keep doubt where the author had doubt: "may", "in one study", "the authors expect".
- Quote only when the exact words matter, and keep a quote under 15 words.
- Do not start with "This document" or "The text discusses". Start with the point.
- If the text contradicts itself, say so instead of choosing one side.

## The answer

Use this shape unless the person asked for another:

- **In one sentence**: the point of the whole text.
- **Key points**: three to six bullets, each a full sentence with its fact.
- **Decisions and next steps**: only if the text has any; who, what, when.
- **Open questions**: only if the text names any.

For a transcript or a meeting, put decisions and next steps first. For a short summary on request
("in two lines"), give only the sentence and the two most important points.
